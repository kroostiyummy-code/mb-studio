A mobile-first single-page website for a Tunisian family restaurant

THE PHILOSOPHY
================================================================

Atmosphere first. Food second. Interface third.

The website must feel like a physical place before it feels
like a website.

After 5 seconds on mobile, the visitor must think:
"J'ai envie d'un couscous là-bas, aujourd'hui à midi."

================================================================
THE PLACE — Atmosphere
================================================================

This is a small, warm Tunisian dining room in the heart of
Chartres. The smell of cumin and slow-simmered tomato reaches
you before you even sit down. The kitchen works in real time —
bricks are fried to order, couscous has been steeping since
morning. Tables are simple. The décor is honest: a few
photographs of Sidi Bou Said on the wall, the blue door of
Tunisia hanging in the memory of the room.

Lighting: Full midday sun. Warm and direct. No drama, no
dimming. The light of a Tuesday lunch in July — natural,
generous, unhurried.

Materials: Warm off-white walls, wooden tables, ceramic
plates, the deep blue of Sidi Bou Said as a visual anchor,
terracotta tones from the spices and the food itself.

Sound: The gentle noise of a family restaurant in service —
plates, a conversation, the sizzle of something frying out
back. Not silence, not a club. A working kitchen.

People: Regulars who come back every week. Families sharing a
couscous royal. A student grabbing a fricassé on the way to
class. A table of four splitting a tajine. The owner who knows
their names.

Time of day: Midday. 12h30. The room is full.

The temperature is warm and solar.
The density is medium-high — lived-in, not crowded.
The rhythm is steady and unhurried — the pace of a real meal,
not fast food, not fine dining.

================================================================
THE PLACE — What it is NOT
================================================================

This is NOT:
- A generic halal fast-food counter with neon signs
- A "Mille et une nuits" folkloric décor with mashrabiya patterns,
  Moroccan zellige tiles, or pan-Arab orientalist aesthetics —
  this is specifically Tunisian, not a generic Middle Eastern cliché
- A gastronomic premium restaurant — no cold marble, no hushed
  white space, no €40+ positioning
- A lounge or nightlife venue — despite evening hours, the soul
  of this place is diurnal and familial
- A kebab or shawarma restaurant — Tunisian cuisine is its own
  tradition, not a variation of Turkish or Lebanese fast food
- A B2B SaaS website (no Stripe / Linear / Notion aesthetic)
- A generic Wix or Shopify restaurant template
- An Apple-style minimalist product page — cold, empty, soulless
- An Instagram food-porn shoot with black backgrounds and
  theatrical lighting
